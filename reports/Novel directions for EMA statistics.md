# Turn EMA statistics into predictive science

The EMA-of-sufficient-statistics update at the heart of e-means is not new. It is stepwise / online EM (Sato & Ishii 2000; Cappé & Moulines 2009), and for conjugate models it is stochastic variational inference. Liang & Klein showed in 2009 that it works across four model families, running 2-10x faster than batch EM. So "the e-means recipe generalises to GMM, HMM, LDA, dictionary learning and ALS" cannot be the program's contribution. Your own finding, that tuned stepwise EM is close behind, confirms this. What the literature does *not* have is a **predictive theory of the step size, batch size and component death for this whole class**, a **cross-family time-to-baseline benchmark**, and a set of **fixed-decay EMA k-means inner loops in deep learning** (VQ tokenizers, SAEs, MoE balancing) that break across batch sizes in documented ways. The three anomalies in your data, all unexplained so far, are your best raw material: LDA needs a 50-100x smaller alpha0, the fitted law saturates at alpha0 = 1 while sweeps pick 0.07-0.27, and runs stop at 5 epochs. The recommended identity is "**scaling laws and failure modes of stochastic-approximation EM**". It rests on a statistic-space noise scale and a timescale reparameterisation of the law that explain those anomalies. Three test beds carry it: new classic model families that stress universality, EMA-VQ codebooks in deep learning, and a differentially private variant where tuning-free hyperparameters matter most. Every "not found" claim below comes from a limited search and must pass the novelty checks listed at the end.

## The update rule is taken; laws, death and benchmarks are open

e-means (arXiv 2611.09501, Meta FAIR, posted 6 Nov 2026) updates per-cluster counts and sums by EMA with a cosine-annealed rate. It revives clusters whose count falls below 0.1x the mean every 5 batches. It fits alpha0 and batch-size laws in (k, B, n, T) on the DEEP dataset only, with R² of 0.95-0.96 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09501v1)). Its related work omits the online-EM lineage: Cappé-Moulines, Sato-Ishii, Liang-Klein, Hoffman and Mairal do not appear in the extracted text. It also mentions no extension to GMM, PQ, dictionary learning or topic models ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09501v1)). The authors have therefore not pre-empted the generalisation. That cuts both ways, because reviewers will call it "stepwise EM renamed" unless the paper states the equivalence up front. The mechanism is exactly Cappé & Moulines' stochastic-approximation recursion on expected sufficient statistics followed by a closed-form M-step ([arXiv 0712.4273](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.4273)). Liang & Klein already reported **2-10x speedups over batch EM** across HMM, multinomial mixture, segmentation and IBM Model 1. On POS tagging, stepwise EM reached **65.4% after two passes vs 57.3% for batch EM after 100 iterations**. They also concluded that the best (step power, mini-batch size) "depends on the task and dataset" ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/N09-1069.pdf)). Online LDA (Hoffman et al. 2010) and online dictionary learning (Mairal et al. 2010) are the per-family precedents ([NeurIPS 2010](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3902-online-learning-for-latent-dirichlet-allocation); [JMLR](https://www.di.ens.fr/~fbach/mairal10a.pdf)). Nguyen, Forbes & McLachlan explicitly list tuning learning rates and batch sizes as future work for mini-batch exponential-family mixtures ([arXiv 1902.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03335)).

Outside evidence also tempers the headline speedups. An independent NumPy reproduction measured **1.51x rather than 10x**, with 1.42% worse MSE, and its first attempt failed without revival ([dev.to](https://dev.to/frankchu/10x-faster-k-means-i-re-ran-it-and-got-15x-24bb)). Exact GPU baselines have also moved: Flash-KMeans reports up to 17.9x end-to-end speedups for exact Lloyd ([arXiv 2603.09229](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09229)), and Flash-GMM fuses the GMM E-step into one pass ([HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.10896.md)). Any wall-clock claim has to beat these, not a naive implementation.

The defensible novelty is therefore narrow but real: the schedule, the revival rule, the hyperparameter laws, and evidence at modern scale. The gap between e-means-specific ingredients and a well-tuned 2009 baseline is the only algorithmic delta a paper can claim, and your data show it is small. That points the program away from "faster EM" and toward "predicting and explaining EM's hyperparameters and failures".

## Theory already explains your three anomalies, if you change coordinates

Online EM near convergence is a natural-gradient step preconditioned by the inverse complete-data Fisher information. Its mean-field Jacobian is I_c⁻¹ times the Hessian of the KL divergence, so its eigenvalues are 1 minus the missing-information fractions. A 1/n step is rate-optimal only above a curvature threshold that is "usually impossible to check". Slower decay plus Polyak-Ruppert averaging is the robust, efficient alternative ([Cappé & Moulines](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.4273)). Batch EM is mirror descent with KL geometry, which makes its rates invariant to reparameterisation ([Kunstner et al., AISTATS 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/kunstner21a.html)). For conjugate models, the SVI natural-gradient step with rate rho is literally an EMA of rescaled statistics ([Hoffman et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.7051)). The e-means alpha is therefore a natural-gradient learning rate, and learning-rate theory applies to it. This has three consequences for your data.

**The LDA gap is a noise problem, not a curvature problem.** Missing information slows the mean dynamics to a rate of roughly alpha times (1 - f). Standard stochastic-approximation reasoning then calls for a *larger*, not smaller, step, so overlap alone cannot explain why LDA wants 50-100x less. The better candidate is the statistic-space signal-to-noise ratio per coordinate. A k-means centroid aggregates about n/k dense points. An LDA topic-word statistic aggregates far fewer tokens per (topic, word) cell, with Poisson-like relative variance, so the EMA must remember many more batches. This is the research notes' hypothesis, not a published result. It has an exact deep-learning analogue in McCandlish et al.'s gradient noise scale, which predicts critical batch size to within an order of magnitude across tasks ([arXiv 1812.06162](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06162)). No sufficient-statistic version of the noise scale was found. The proposal is to measure B_noise^S = tr(Σ_s)/‖s̄ − S‖² in the Fisher metric, which costs one extra variance accumulator, and to test whether alpha* ≈ min(1, B/B_noise^S) and B* ≈ B_noise^S.

**The saturation at alpha0 = 1 is a mis-specified functional form.** AdamW weights are an EMA whose optimal timescale, measured in epochs, is roughly constant across model and data size ([Wang & Aitchison, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13698v3)). Bergsma et al. refine this to tau = B/(eta·lambda·D), a power law in tokens per parameter, with optimal batch scaling in D independently of model size ([arXiv 2505.13738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13738)). The e-means EMA has the same structure: its memory in epochs is tau = B/(alpha·n). Fitting log tau against log(data per sufficient-statistic coordinate) has two advantages over fitting alpha0 directly. First, the boundary disappears, since alpha = min(1, B/(tau·n)) is derived rather than clipped. Second, the LDA gap becomes a prediction: data per coordinate is n/(k·d) for k-means but n·L/(K·V) for LDA, where L is document length and V is vocabulary size. Busbridge et al.'s rule for scaling an EMA with batch size (momentum rho → rho^kappa) is the closest single-average precedent ([arXiv 2307.13813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13813)). The K-component generalisation, in which each component sees about B·p_k samples per step, is unpublished as far as the search found.

**The 5-epoch cutoff hides the asymptotic regime, and the schedule depends on it.** Cosine annealing needs the horizon T as an input, and the law takes T as an argument. Schedule-free optimisation removes the horizon by interpolating between primal averaging and Polyak-Ruppert averaging, keeps worst-case guarantees, and was the core of the winning AlgoPerf 2024 self-tuning entry ([Defazio et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15682v2)). A statistic-space version is natural, because the EMA is already a primal average. Constant-step stochastic approximation also carries an O(step) bias that two-step Richardson-Romberg extrapolation cancels ([Dieuleveut, Durmus & Bach](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06386v1)). Both are principled replacements for the cosine tail, and neither has been applied to EM in anything the search found. The cutoff also leaves a known risk untested: e-means itself reports test MSE rising in late epochs in some high-dimensional synthetic cases ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09501v1)). Longer runs are needed before claiming that EMA matches converged batch EM rather than batch EM at 25 iterations.

Three more theory items are provable and look open. The first is **variance-reduced EM at matched 1-3 epochs**. SPIDER-EM needs n + √n/ε E-step evaluations and suggests a few online-EM epochs before switching ([arXiv 2012.01929](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01929)), but no paper compares sEM-vr, FIEM or SPIDER-EM against constant-step EMA at a few-epoch budget. The second is an **EMA termination theorem**. Schwartzman proves that memoryless mini-batch k-means terminates in O(d/ε) iterations when B = Ω̃((d/ε)²) ([arXiv 2304.00419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.00419)), and an EMA with effective batch about 2B/alpha should weaken the batch requirement, at the cost of a staleness term. The third is a **dead-cluster theory**. Unused counts decay as (1−alpha)^t, and a cluster of mass p sees zero hits with probability (1−p)^{Bt}. That gives a closed-form tradeoff between false and true revival, and implies the revival threshold should scale with alpha and B, not stay a constant 0.1x mean.

The aim of new classic tracks is not to add more "it also works on X" results, which Liang & Klein already provide. It is to span statistic types that break or confirm a normalised law. Some candidates are poor investments. Streaming PCA is well theorised: block power methods already average within mini-batches ([NeurIPS 2013](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/file/76cf99d3614e23eabab16fb27e944bf9-Paper.pdf)). Mini-batch kernel k-means already exists with an O(log k) guarantee ([arXiv 2410.05902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05902)). Archetypal analysis already sits inside the online dictionary-learning framework ([ECCV 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCV_2018/html/Jieru_Mei_Online_Dictionary_Learning_ECCV_2018_paper.html)). CRFs, mean shift and Soft-Impute lack the closed-form statistics the recipe needs. SOM, neural gas and LBG belong in related work as historical precursors, not as tracks. The strategic choice is three or four tracks with different statistic geometries: continuous low-rank (MFA), directional (vMF), multilinear (CP-ALS) and discrete psychometric (IRT). Add PLSA as the targeted ablation that explains LDA.

## Deep-learning inner loops still run fixed-decay EMA k-means

The highest-impact external application is VQ codebook training, which is EMA k-means with ad hoc restarts. VQ-VAE codebooks are "an exponentially smoothed version of k-means" ([arXiv 1806.10474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.10474)). The widely used vector-quantize-pytorch library defaults to decay 0.8, and published setups use 0.9-0.99 with no stated rule ([GitHub](https://github.com/lucidrains/vector-quantize-pytorch)). A September 2026 issue documents the exact unit bug that a per-component scaling law would fix. The dead-code threshold is compared against an EMA of per-batch counts whose scale is vectors_per_step/K. A threshold of 2, copied from large-batch configs, reset **867-1011 of 1024 codes per layer per step**. In a real speech codec, changing the threshold from 2 to 0.2 lowered Whisper WER at 0.5 kbps **from 14.5% to 11.9%** ([Issue #257](https://github.com/lucidrains/vector-quantize-pytorch/issues/257)). VQGAN-LC finds that EMA VQ stops improving at a codebook size of about 16,384 ([NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/1716d022edeac750e57a2986a7135e13-Paper-Conference.pdf)). That fits the per-code effective sample rate falling as K grows at fixed batch. Meanwhile, 2024-2026 work mostly abandons EMA: FSQ, LFQ, SimVQ, the rotation trick and IBQ, and in 2026 NS-VQ/TransVQ, which diagnoses encoder non-stationarity and uses a fixed-alpha EMA baseline with no schedule or restarts ([arXiv 2602.18896](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18896v1)). The contrarian thesis, "EMA done right, with cosine-annealed decay, count-relative revival and a (K, batch, steps) law, is competitive with reparameterisation fixes", is novel as far as the search found. The risk is that SimVQ and IBQ already report near-full utilisation, so a win must show up in rFID, bitrate, scaling to large K, or robustness across batch sizes. The closest prior art for revival is CVQ-VAE's usage-based re-anchoring ([arXiv 2307.15139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15139)).

Sparse autoencoders are the deep-learning twin of the dictionary-learning track. Gao et al. report up to **90% dead latents** without mitigation, cut to 7% at 16M latents with AuxK and decoder-transpose init, and dead-ness is defined by a fixed token window ([arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04093)). A 2026 ICML paper traces death at initialisation to activation outliers, with Spearman correlation 0.89 across 454 model-layer pairs, and fixes it with mean-centering, not resampling ([arXiv 2605.31518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31518)). Two openings remain. One is a batch- and dictionary-size-relative EMA firing-frequency rule for death during training, which complements mean-centering. The other is a Mairal-style closed-form decoder update from EMA statistics A = E[zzᵀ] and B = E[xzᵀ] as a cheap SAE trainer. ITDA shows the appetite for non-SGD dictionaries: about 1% of SAE training cost, though with worse reconstruction ([arXiv 2505.17769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17769)). The risk is that the interpretability community scores on interpretability benchmarks, not reconstruction speed.

Three other loops are weaker fits. DeepSeek's auxiliary-loss-free MoE balancing updates a per-expert bias with a fixed-rate sign step ([arXiv 2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15664)). Han & Zhong now frame this as primal-dual with log regret ([arXiv 2512.03915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03915)). An annealed, per-expert-normalised rate is plausibly novel, but credible evidence needs at least 1B-scale MoE runs, beyond one 5090. SSL prototypes are largely pre-empted: an ICLR 2026 paper already decouples DINO prototypes as an online-EM GMM ([arXiv 2510.20108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20108)), against a backdrop where only 1.6% of 65,536 DINO prototypes are unique ([arXiv 2410.14060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14060)). KV-cache clustering (ClusterKV, CentroidKV, STARC) fits the drift tracks but is crowded with systems papers, and whether CentroidKV already uses running-mean centroids is unverified ([arXiv 2412.03213](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03213); [arXiv 2506.11418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11418)). The unifying contribution across all of these is the **per-component EMA scaling rule**, Busbridge's batch rule generalised to count-weighted statistics with effective batch B·p_k. It explains why VQ dead-code thresholds and SAE dead windows both break when batch size changes.

## Privacy and systems are where hyperparameter laws pay off most

A law matters most where tuning is expensive or forbidden. Under differential privacy every tuning run spends budget, so the private setting is where a predictive law gives the most leverage. Central DP k-means is still noisy Lloyd. Each iteration releases noisy per-cluster sums and counts over the full data, so privacy loss grows **linearly in iterations** ([Su et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05998v1)). The state of the art, FastLloyd (USENIX Security 2025), adds secure aggregation and size constraints but remains full-pass ([arXiv 2405.02437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02437v2)). Subsampling amplification has been used for DP k-means only as a one-shot importance sample ([arXiv 2307.10187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10187v3)). DP-PCA shows that single-pass minibatch Gaussian-mechanism updates can be near-optimal ([arXiv 2205.13709](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13709)). No paper was found combining Poisson-subsampled mini-batches, an EMA over noisy statistics and an RDP accountant. That combination is **DP-e-means**. In it, the annealed rate doubles as a noise-averaging schedule, revival must itself be private (re-seeding from a data point leaks it), and the law gains a privacy axis (alpha0, B, sigma) = f(n, k, epsilon). It is cheap to run on one GPU, reuses the GMM code for a DP-GMM variant, and composes with secure aggregation because sums and counts are additive. The search also points to continual-release DP streaming clustering ([Epasto et al., AISTATS 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/epasto26a.html)), to which a decayed-sum matrix mechanism is a natural EMA counterpart.

Three systems directions are narrow but concrete. **Low-precision EMA accumulation**: under cosine annealing, the late increment alpha·b can fall below half an ulp of S in bf16 and be absorbed, freezing the statistics exactly at the end of training. Mixed-precision Lloyd has been analysed ([arXiv 2407.12208](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12208)), but the interaction with a decaying rate has not. Remedies to test include stochastic rounding, Kahan compensation and a precision-dependent rate floor. This is directly relevant to Blackwell FP8. **Query-weighted EMA partitions**: SPFresh, Ada-IVF and Quake maintain IVF partitions with discrete split/merge driven by access temperature. Quake reports 1.5-38x lower query latency on dynamic workloads ([arXiv 2506.03437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03437)). Learned routers sit on fixed k-means partitions ([arXiv 2404.11731](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11731v1)). An EMA over the query stream, or a separately trained routing centroid pulled toward missed queries, is a continuous alternative. It overlaps the live drifting IVF track, so it should be framed as an extension. **One-pass out-of-core IVF training**: cuVS trains on about 10% subsamples with balanced hierarchical k-means ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-vector-search-nvidia-cuvs-ivf-pq-deep-dive-part-1/)), and Ada-IVF notes billion-scale rebuilds can take days ([arXiv 2411.00970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00970v1)). A single streamed epoch with law-chosen batch size, built on Flash-KMeans' fused kernels, is a clear experiment. Clustering-based curation at billion scale ([Vo et al., TMLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15613)) is high-impact but needs expensive downstream evaluation. Non-private federated clustering ([k-FED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00697)) is incremental on its own; its one new piece is participation-aware revival, since a cluster can look dead only because its clients skipped a round.

## Recommended identity: a predictive theory of stochastic-approximation EM

The flagship should claim what 2009-era work lacked. A suggested framing is "**Stochastic-approximation EM at scale: a noise-scale law, failure modes, and a time-to-baseline benchmark**". It names stepwise EM and e-means as two points in one design space: schedule shape × step scale × batch × revival × averaging. It makes tuned Liang-Klein stepwise EM the primary baseline rather than a threat. Its central scientific claim is falsifiable: optimal alpha0 and B are predicted by a measurable statistic-space noise scale, or equivalently by EMA memory in epochs against data per statistic coordinate, and this collapses k-means, GMM, HMM, ALS, dictionary learning and LDA onto one curve. Where the collapse fails, missing information or M-step nonlinearity is the identified residual. The LDA gap and the alpha0 = 1 saturation become evidence for the theory instead of embarrassments. The deep-learning and privacy tracks then serve as high-visibility demonstrations that the law and revival theory transfer where practitioners currently hand-tune.

| Priority | New track | Deliverable | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Statistic-space noise scale + timescale law | Refit existing sweeps as log tau vs log(data per coordinate); measure B_noise^S; PLSA vs LDA ablation | Uses data you already have; directly explains LDA and saturation |
| 2 | Horizon-free averaging | Schedule-free / tail-averaged / Richardson-Romberg EM vs cosine, runs past 5 epochs, plus VR-EM at matched epochs | Removes T from the law; closes the 5-epoch gap |
| 3 | Unified death theory | Closed-form revival threshold in (alpha, B, p_k); one operator across k-means, GMM, LDA, NMF, VQ | No unified treatment found; Issue #257 is live evidence |
| 4 | EMA-VQ done right | Cosine decay + relative revival + law vs FSQ/SimVQ/rotation/IBQ on an image tokenizer and an RVQ codec | Contrarian, high-visibility, fits one GPU at small scale |
| 5 | DP-e-means / DP-GMM-EMA | Subsampled noisy-statistics EMA with RDP accounting, private revival, privacy-aware law vs FastLloyd | Clearest gap found; tuning costs privacy |
| 6 | Universality stress tests | MFA, vMF, CP-ALS, IRT as law test points | Different statistic geometries; thin online prior art |
| 7 | Low-precision EMA accumulation | bf16/fp8 absorption analysis and remedies on a fused kernel | Narrow but new, cheap, 5090-native |

The benchmark (time and epochs to batch-EM@25, across families, with fixed per-family baselines) should ship as the paper's artifact, not as a separate identity. Analogous neural benchmarks exist, but no cross-family time-to-target suite for latent-variable models was found.

## Conclusion

Your anomalies say the e-means law sits in the wrong coordinates, not that EMA-of-statistics learners lack a law. Liang & Klein's 17-year-old observation that optimal settings are task-dependent has never been explained, and natural-gradient theory, the gradient noise scale and EMA timescale laws together supply the tools to explain it. A program that turns "task-dependent" into "predicted from a measurable noise scale and data per statistic coordinate" owns a question rather than an algorithm. That is harder to pre-empt than any single new track.

The second insight is that the same per-component batch-scaling error recurs across communities that do not read each other: VQ dead-code thresholds, SAE dead-latent windows, MoE bias rates and k-means revival. A single theory of when EMA statistics starve a component, validated on a classic model and one deep-learning loop, gives the program an identity e-means does not have and reviewers cannot call stepwise EM renamed. Running the refit on existing sweeps first is cheap and decisive: if the timescale coordinates collapse k-means and LDA, the flagship is in hand; if they do not, the residual itself points to missing information or M-step nonlinearity as the next result.
